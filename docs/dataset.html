Dataset

TR-6: six produce types, three sensors, fresh to spoiled

The TriModal Ripeness 6 dataset follows individual fruits and vegetables day by day as they degrade after harvest, recording each one with a colour camera, a thermal camera and a methane gas sensor.

What was recorded

Each specimen was photographed and thermally imaged in three sessions a day (morning, afternoon and evening) and its methane emission was measured in the same sessions, from a fresh starting condition until it was judged spoiled. For modelling, the sessions of a day are combined into one day-level entry, and the days of a specimen form a trajectory.

sRGB Colour photographs, resized to 224 × 224 for the model
Thermal Infrared fusion images on the same session schedule
Gas Methane in ppm: mean, spread, left and right sensor

111 of the 112 day-level entries have all three modalities; the remaining one has both images but no gas reading. Individual sessions are missing more often (up to about 10% of evening sessions), which is why the gas encoder is built to handle gaps and the fusion layer can leave a sensor out.

Why the regression task is hard

Tomato's mean of 31.33 days until spoilage is about 4.5 times that of the next produce, and its range of 0–65 days dwarfs the others. One regression head has to fit all six scales at once. Three target rescalings were tried; none helped, and all of them hurt classification. Details are on the Results page.

How the data is split

Splits are made per (produce, label) stratum and in time order: the earliest 70% of a specimen's days go to training, the next 15% to validation and the most recent 15% to testing. The model therefore never trains on a day that comes after a day it is tested on.

What this means for the results

Every split contains days from every specimen, so the headline test score measures how well the model handles later days of specimens it has partly seen. Generalising to a completely new produce type is tested separately with leave-one-fruit-out, where results are much weaker. The not-spoiled carrot has only two days, so it has no test day, and the test set covers 11 of the 12 trajectories.

Labels

Each day-level entry carries a binary spoiled / not-spoiled label and a days-until-spoilage value counted back from the day the specimen was judged spoiled. The model classifies a whole trajectory using its last day, so each test trajectory counts once however many days it has.
