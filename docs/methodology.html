Methodology

How the system is built, and how it was tested

Three encoders, one per sensor, feed a shared fusion and temporal stage, which feeds three task heads. Select any block to see what it does.

Architecture

32.47 million parameters · 39.03 GFLOPs per forward pass on a batch of four trajectories · 36.9 ms per batch on an Apple Silicon laptop. Open the detailed architecture figure.

How a missing sensor is handled

For each day the three sensor embeddings become three tokens. Attention lets them inform each other, and a key-padding mask removes any sensor that did not report that day. The fused vector is the mean of the available tokens only, so a missing sensor never contributes a placeholder value.

Tt=[vtrgb;vtth;vtgas]∈ℝ3×d (Zt,αt)=MultiHeadAttn(Q=Tt,K=Tt,V=Tt;mask=¬availt) zt=1|At|∑k∈AtLayerNorm(Tt+Zt)k

At is the set of sensors available on day t, each vt is a 64-dimensional sensor embedding, and the mask removes unavailable sensors from the attention keys.

How gaps in the gas readings are handled

GRU-D keeps the elapsed time δt since the last reading and a mask mt. When a reading is missing it blends the last value with the training mean, and it learns how quickly the old value should fade. The mask is also an input, so missingness itself is information.

γx=exp(−max(0,Wγxδt+bγx)) x^t=mt⊙xt+(1−mt)⊙(γx⊙xt−1+(1−γx)⊙x¯) ht=GRUCell([x^t;mt],γh⊙ht−1)

δt is the time since the last reading, mt is 1 when a reading exists, x̄ is the training mean, and ⊙ is element-wise multiplication. γh decays the hidden state the same way γx decays the input.

Training recipe

OptimiserAdamW, weight decay 1e-4, cosine schedule over 30 epochs
Learning rate1e-4; thermal backbone 1e-5
Batch size4 trajectories, gradient clipping at norm 1.0
Backbone unfreezinglast block at epoch 6, last two at 8, all at 12
Regularisationdropout 0.5; one flipped and rotated (±15°) copy of each training trajectory
Class imbalanceclass weight 2.3 on spoiled; weighted random sampler
Seeds10 independent models (seeds 0–9), probabilities averaged
Threshold6-fold median over training + validation predictions: 0.660

Evaluation approach

With 12 specimens, a single train/test number means little on its own. Four practices run through every investigation.

The test set decides nothing

Architecture, hyperparameters and the decision threshold are all chosen without the test set. Per-epoch thresholds are calibrated on training data, never on validation.

Paired bootstrap significance testing

Trajectories are resampled 10,000 times with the same indices for both models being compared, giving a 95% interval and a p-value for their difference, not just two numbers.

Correction for many comparisons

29 pairwise comparisons are reported across the project, so a Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate correction is applied across all of them.

A second, independent check

A six-fold cross-validation that retrains the model in every fold, a trivial always-spoiled baseline, and two independently trained runs of the final model show how much of any difference is ordinary training noise.

Investigations & findings

Roughly in the order they were carried out. Full numbers are on the Results page.

  1. 01

    Ablation study

    Seven configurations: the full model, four single-component ablations (no CBAM, no cross-attention, LSTM instead of Transformer, mean imputation instead of GRU-D) and two baselines (early and late fusion). Early and late fusion and the no-cross-attention variant scored 0.09–0.13 lower F1, while two simplified variants scored higher.

  2. 02

    Bootstrap significance on the ablation

    None of the six alternatives differs significantly from the full model. The ranking in the raw ablation table is not something this sample size can support.

  3. 03

    Backbone benchmark

    Eight sRGB backbones, including a frozen DINOv2. ConvNeXt-Tiny had the highest mean F1, but its margin is not significant, and EfficientNet-B0 came close at a fraction of the cost.

  4. 04

    Missing-sensor robustness

    Each sensor, and all three jointly, switched off on 0–50% of days. The model kept the highest F1 in most single-sensor conditions; its advantage under gas and thermal failure survives correction, while sRGB failure hurts it most.

  5. 05

    Leave-one-fruit-out generalisation

    Six folds, each holding out one produce type entirely. The ensemble got both held-out trajectories right in only 1 of 6 folds, the most serious limitation found.

  6. 06

    Explainability: Grad-CAM, SHAP and attention

    All three test errors were borderline false alarms. Grad-CAM showed some attention on the background; SHAP showed the model relies on mean methane concentration; the gas sensor receives the least attention weight.

  7. 07

    The CBAM question, resolved directly

    Grad-CAM raised the question of whether CBAM helps, so a model without CBAM on either camera was trained twice from scratch and evaluated on test. Both runs were worse on every test metric. The difference is not significant, so CBAM is kept on consistency across two runs.

  8. 08

    Uncertainty quantification

    Bootstrap intervals for every headline metric and a six-fold retraining cross-validation. Intervals are wide, as expected from 11 test trajectories.

  9. 09

    Shelf-life regression: diagnosis and three fixes

    The regression head (test R² 0.04) cannot even fit its training data, because tomato's timescale is an order of magnitude longer than the rest. Per-produce z-score, fraction of shelf life and log transform were each trained and tested; all three failed and lowered classification F1.

  10. 10

    Capacity re-sweep

    Embedding size and dropout were searched again for the ConvNeXt-Tiny model. No setting beat the defaults (64-d, dropout 0.5) under the full ten-seed protocol, so they were kept.
